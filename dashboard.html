# SPEND LEDGER The revenue side of this program has a daily history. The spend side did not, which meant every question about whether money was working got answered from whatever window happened to be in front of us. This file fixes that. Two ledgers, one derived view. ## The two ledgers **`data/ads_daily.csv`** - one row per date per campaign. Append-only. Cost, impressions, clicks, CTR, average CPC, and conversions split by action. Cost settles within about a day, so it is pulled **every run**, not just on sweep runs. Conversions carry a `settled_as_of` date and a `restated_from` value when a later pull revises them. **`data/budget_changes.csv`** - the change-point ledger. Every budget, bidding, geo, schedule, status, or asset change, with who made it. This is what turns a flat spend series into comparable regimes. Rows carry a `confidence` field: `confirmed` means it appears in a session log with a date, `inferred` means the value is known but the exact change date is not. The `source` field matters as much as the value. `Arda` is a decision. `Google` is something that happened to the account. `Billing` is an outage. In April, Google added 11 sitelinks on its own and about $914 went out the door before they were removed. That belongs in the same ledger as a deliberate budget increase, because from the revenue side it is indistinguishable. ## Budget is not spend Never report budget as if it were spend. Google over-delivers against a daily budget and settles against a monthly average. Boxx has run over budget repeatedly: May 2 spent $55.55 against a $45 budget, May 5 spent $54.76. Both figures belong in the record. Every run reports **budgeted daily** and **actual spend** side by side, plus the delivery ratio. A campaign persistently under-delivering is a different problem from one persistently over- delivering, and both are invisible if you only look at the budget field. ## The derived view: spend regimes A **regime** is the span between two change points for a campaign, or account-wide between two account-level change points. For each regime, compute and carry forward: | Field | Definition | |---|---| | Regime start, end, days | bounded by change points, outages break a regime | | Budgeted daily, total budgeted | from the change ledger | | Actual spend, spend per day | from `ads_daily.csv` | | Delivery ratio | actual spend / budgeted | | Daytime tx per day | day-of-week matched where the regime is shorter than three weeks | | Net sales per day | Square | | Ad cost as percent of net sales | the number that tells you whether this is affordable | | Cost per transaction at the margin | change in spend per day divided by change in tx per day against the prior clean regime | | Clean | true only if the regime overlaps no row in `CONTAMINATION_LOG.md` | Report the regime table every run. It is short, it accumulates, and it is the only artifact that answers "did spending more ever produce more." ## The honest caveat, which must appear beside the regime table every time Cost per marginal transaction computed this way is **not incrementality**. It is a before-and-after across a change point, and this business has already demonstrated why that is weak evidence: the Jun 5 to 10 suspension put spend at zero and daytime transactions barely moved, ads-on 215.8 against ads-off 218.8. Accumulated audience effects persist after spend stops. So the regime table answers a narrower question honestly: **at each level of spend we have tried, what did the business actually do.** Treat a favourable marginal cost as a reason to test further, never as proof the spend caused the revenue. If a regime is not `clean`, say so in the same sentence as the number. ## Program to date Carry three cumulative figures in `state.json` and show them every run: - Total ad spend since 2026-03-21 - Total spend by campaign, including campaigns now paused or divested - Ad cost as a percent of net sales across the whole program The last one is the number that matters at partner level. Mert and Altug are not going to read a trigger table, and the program has now been running long enough that "what has this cost and what came back" deserves a standing answer rather than a reconstruction each time it is asked. ## What this cannot tell you None of this establishes causation, and no amount of accumulated daily data will. The only path to real verification of in-store sales against ad spend is a **Store Sales offline conversion import**, uploading Square transactions back to Google Ads. That remains a project, not a task, and it stays in `state.json > known_blockers` until it is scoped. The ledger is the best available substitute in the meantime, and it should describe itself that way in every output.
